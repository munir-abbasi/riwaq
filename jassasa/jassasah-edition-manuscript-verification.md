# Jassāsah: supplementary edition and manuscript verification audit

**Date:** 27 October 2026. **Scope:** selected previously unresolved digital citations and one additional witness found while checking al-Ṭabarānī. This is an incremental audit, not a comprehensive source census or manuscript collation. The pre-existing nineteen-entry study is a *frozen analytical sample*.

## Evidence definitions

- **D (direct digital text):** Arabic report actually opened and checked against the identifying chain or diagnostic reading at the linked host. The web transcription may derive from a printed edition, but its source exemplar has **not** been inspected.
- **B (bibliographic locator only):** edition-style volume/page or report identifier displayed by host, without verified editor/publisher/title page or actual page image.
- **P (printed exemplar inspected):** an identifiable printed page image and title page examined. **None achieved.**
- **M (manuscript reading inspected):** legible folio with a verified holding/shelfmark and contextual report reading examined. **None achieved.**
- **U (not independently reopened in this supplementary pass):** earlier retained transcription may exist; it does not establish new verification.

## Consequences for the article

1. The earlier claim that the nineteen entries exhaust the specifically enumerated sample is valid **only as a statement of frozen selection**, not complete nearby-source coverage. At least al-Ṭabarānī 964 and the newly noticed 969 require inventory decisions.
2. Persia language now has a directly reopened *third displayed source* within the Dāwūd–Ḥammād family (al-Ṭabarānī 964), alongside the existing Aḥmad and al-Nasāʾī transcriptions. This strengthens documentation of that shared lower-route reading, **without providing a new independent pupil branch** or proving the feature's origin.
3. Edition-style page numbers in digital displays are **locators**, not verified publication metadata. A numbering crosswalk must not be asserted between Aḥmad 26066/27102, Ibn Abī Shaybah 40425/38791, or al-Ṭayālisī 1751/1756 without viewing the referenced editions.
4. Manuscript evidence remains **zero verified folios**, and no dated material witness has been placed in the stemma. No claim of manuscript corroboration or precise textual chronology is defensible.
5. The Riwaq dataset still consists of **19 selected digital attestations, 18 encoded records, 20 parsed paths**. Neither new al-Ṭabarānī lead has been encoded. Any augmented result requires source-form chain segmentation, provenance retention, import validation and recomputation.

## Unresolved work and falsification targets

For each of the nineteen baseline attestations and any admitted additions, retrieve identifiable printed editions, inspect their **title pages and the relevant report page images**, and record editor, imprint, year, volume/page, chapter and number. Where two editions differ, transcribe both readings rather than silently preferring a numbered website. For manuscript investigation, first identify collection, shelfmark, codicological date and matching book/section; only then inspect and cite the actual folios. Catalog descriptions alone are not textual witnesses. Recheck the full 964/969 isnāds, al-Ṭabarānī 967's conjunction, the biographical identities, and the dependence between digital host transcriptions. There is currently **no evidence from inspected manuscripts** that would warrant modifying the article's textual clustering or dating hypotheses.

## Reproducibility note

The directly checked links above are web transcriptions, accessed through a searchable public web index and opened source pages on 27 October 2026. They are not photographs of printed pages. Searches for manuscript-level witnesses produced no verified folio that could be assigned securely to any of these exact report attestations. The record deliberately distinguishes a displayed book number or page from verified edition metadata.
